Obituaries

In memory of those who played their part in Lamma Cricket Club.

In Memoriam

Clive Theobald

One of the original Lamma CC team from 1992, Clive passed away on New Year’s Eve after a battle with heart disease. He was a very good friend of John Fox and Dick Jones, who passed away four years earlier. A founding spirit of this club.

In Memoriam

Richard “Dick” Jones

We were saddened to hear of the passing of Richard “Dick” Jones on 27 November 2014, in his sleep at home on Lamma.

Dick was one of the original Lamma CC players before the Club entered the Saturday League and was the first to hold the title of Lamma’s “Best American Cricketer”. A professional photographer and cameraman, he was an enthusiastic, friendly character with a wealth of stories, personal adventures and things to talk about. Dick went on several Club tours, mostly in the 1990s, as a player and supporter. Many will know him for his annual “Dickstock” party featuring local bands held in his garden as late into the night as law enforcement allowed. He also featured in Howard Marks’ autobiography.

Dick was one of life’s characters and good guys, and will be sadly missed. Raise a beer for him. RIP Dick.
In Memoriam

Richie Benaud

Lamma CC was saddened to hear of the passing of former Australia captain and legendary cricket commentator Richie Benaud at the age of 84. A pioneering leg-spin bowler, Benaud played 63 Tests (28 as captain) before retiring in 1964 to pursue a career in journalism and broadcasting. His final commentary in England came during the famous 2005 Ashes series.

“Morning everyone.”
In Memoriam

Phillip Hughes

Cricket fans and players around the world used their bats to pay tribute to Australian cricketer Phillip Hughes, who passed away at 25 after being struck in the neck during a domestic match in Australia. Thousands posted photographs under #putoutyourbats and #putyourbatout — professionals, former players and amateurs alike — in one of cricket’s most moving outpourings of respect.

“People are putting bats in their back yard and in their front window. We thank them for that.” — Cricket Australia CEO James Sutherland
