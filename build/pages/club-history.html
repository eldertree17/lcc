Since 1990

The Club History

From a disused helipad to League Champions — the story of Lamma Cricket Club so far (recorded up to 2004).

A long time ago, on an island far away… A Lamma cricket team played its first game in 1990 when some players were invited to play against the Hong Kong University Social XI at Sandy Bay — a tradition that survives to this day as an annual fixture between the two sides. In 1992, Lamma’s growing community began to include keen cricketers, leading to the formation of a more organised club. The Lamma Cricket Club was inaugurated at a beery meeting at the original Waterfront Restaurant, and the first practice session was conducted on a disused helipad — one of the few areas of ‘flat’ land on the island. Not that flat though: the first the batsman saw of the bowler was his head appearing over the horizon from his uphill run-up!
1990–1992

Humble beginnings

At that time the Club’s meagre supply of equipment consisted simply of one bat, one ball and some tatty old pads that came from the discredited bank of BCCI. As we became more organised we received sponsorship from the Island Bar, which helped us afford equipment and team shirts, and we started to look like a proper cricket team. The practice facilities improved a little when we transferred to a site adjacent to the football pitch on Yung Shue Wan harbour.

The Logo

That lovely logo

The cricket club logo incorporates the map of Lamma Island and the chimneys of the Island’s chief landmark, the power station. Interestingly, there were only two chimneys when they were used as the inspiration for the stumps; whether HK Electric added a third to match our logo has not yet been confirmed. The colours come from the green of the Island and the yellow from the stumps (beige was considered too wishy-washy).

1993–2001

Lamma roams around the region

As grounds became increasingly difficult to find and our enthusiasm grew, we began to tour. Our tours have included Manila, in the Philippines (played at the Nomads ground in 1993); Bangkok, Thailand (at the Royal Bangkok Sports Club in 1994); and Colombo, Kandy and Kalutara in Sri Lanka in 1995.

During the tour to Sri Lanka the opposition got stronger while the pitches deteriorated and injuries grew and grew. An account of this “Tour from Hell” even made the pages of the South China Morning Post (read the article).

A marching band paraded around the empty stadium — one of the most bizarre things to happen on a Lamma C.C. tour. And that is really saying something!

In a return to the Philippines in 1995, we visited Angeles City where we won the First Mount Pinatubo Ashes Sixes Tournament. The tour generated interest from the local tourist board, keen to promote the area after the American Air Force pulled out of nearby Subic Bay. The match was played in a disused American football stadium, and we were honoured with a marching band. In 1998 Lamma C.C. were the sole representatives for Hong Kong at the Bangkok Invitational Sixes Tournament and reached a very creditable quarter final amongst high-quality opposition. Later that year we toured Perth, Australia, taking in an Australia vs England Ashes Test match. In 2001 we visited Vietnam, playing two matches against what looked like the Sri Lankan national team — one inside a greyhound stadium, a second at Hanoi’s air force base. More recently we have played sixes events in Shanghai, China, and Chiang Mai, Thailand, at the world’s biggest amateur cricket sixes tournament.

1994–2004

Review of the seasons

Early games and the first full social season did not meet with much (any, if the truth be told) success, but we persevered, encouraged by a love of the game and the undeniable realisation that we could do no worse. As players improved and new ones joined, we began to win games — the most successful social season being 1994–95, when we won every social game and reached the quarter final of the Saturday Cup before losing by one run to the eventual Cup winners.

A further reduction in grounds in 1995 meant fewer opportunities to play. To keep the Club focused, we built our own cricket net along the Yung Shue Wan harbourfront. It lasted long enough for Lamma C.C. to achieve admission to the Saturday League in September 1996, just in time for the 1996–97 season. The Club remained firmly at the foot of the table that season, but we were learning fast — and won the Craigengower CC Invitational Sixes Tournament at the Kowloon Cricket Club. Not bad for supposed wooden-spooners!

We did better the following season, 1997–98, finishing joint 10th and again reaching the Saturday Cup quarter final. During the summer of 1998 we entered two teams in the Summer Eights competition and won the league against some of Hong Kong’s leading clubs. In 1999 we won the HKU Invitational Sixes and reached the final of the Saturday Cup. The 1998–99 season ended with a highly creditable 4th place — a vastly improved campaign that earned Brad Tarr the “Captain of the Year” accolade from the Hong Kong Cricket Association.

Season 1999–2000 went relatively well with 6th place, while 2000–01 brought a reversal of fortunes amid squad and injury struggles, ending mid-table. The season of 2001–02 saw Lamma C.C. reach their then highest position — 3rd in the Saturday League — including a record-breaking string of eight wins in a row. Then, in 2002–03, League Champions! We got our reward for consistently good cricket, also winning the Plate in Shanghai, reaching the Chiang Mai Sixes quarter-finals and the Summer Eights semi-finals. We defended the title hard in 2003–04, finishing a tight 4th, again winning the Plate in Shanghai and becoming the only team to beat the eventual Chiang Mai champions. September 2004 saw a tour to Shanghai’s International Sixes, guest-starring Viv Richards and Doug Walters.

Off the field

Strictly ballroom & by the way

The summer of 1999 saw the inaugural Lamma C.C. black tie Dinner & Dance, held at Oscar’s in Causeway Bay — a time for award presentations, dressing up and having a good time. It proved a big event and went so well it became a yearly Lamma “event”. The second Dinner & Dance moved to the Century Hotel in Wanchai, a bigger venue for the increasingly popular night, with a buffet dinner, award presentations and performances from three bands.

And by the way: even though Lamma C.C. does not have the attraction of its own ground, it has a large and keen membership — and it is the only Club without a ground to field both a League Team and a Social Team.
